# Schematic drawing-style pass (generic, for any KiCad project)

> Use this after the schematic is electrically complete and verified (ERC clean, netlist rules passing).
> It changes **only the drawing**: placement, wiring, labels, text positions, sheet grouping. It must never change
> connectivity, part values, references or footprints. The whole file can be pasted as a prompt for Claude Code.
>
> Tools in this skill: `scripts/check_connectivity.py` (section 0), `scripts/fix_passive_text.py` (section 3a),
> `scripts/kicad_text.py` (text widths, sections 3a, 4, 4a), `scripts/check_drawing.py` (section 9),
> `scripts/check_structure.py` (sections 5 and 8a, section 9a).

---

<role>
You are a schematic draughtsman working on a verified KiCad schematic. Your job is to make every sheet readable
and printable: no overlapping text, components wired to the parts they serve, clear inter-sheet interfaces and a
sensible sheet structure. You do not change the electrical design.
</role>

<context>
- The schematic is the source of truth for connectivity; the current netlist is the reference.
- If the schematic is generated by a script, change the generator, never the generated files by hand.
- Everything written into the schematic is in English.
</context>

<instructions>

## 0. Before you start

1. Export the netlist of the current commit and save it as the **reference netlist** (`build/reference_<commit>.net`).
2. Use the **connectivity-equivalence check** `scripts/check_connectivity.py <reference.net> <current.net>`: it
   compares pin groups (sets of `ref.pin` that share a net) between the reference netlist and the new one,
   ignoring net names. Pin 1/2 swaps on non-polarized two-terminal parts (R, C, L, fuses, bidirectional TVS) count
   as equivalent. Run it after every step; any difference is a drawing bug (a wire shorted something or left
   something open), not an acceptable change.
3. Use the **drawing-clearance check** `scripts/check_drawing.py` on the plotted PDF (see section 9) and the
   **R/C text sweep** `scripts/fix_passive_text.py --check` (section 3a). Add both to the pre-commit verification.

## 1. No overlaps anywhere

On every sheet, nothing may overlap. That covers:

- **Text on text:** reference, value, pin name/number, label, power-symbol name, note.
- **Text on wires:** a wire crossing a reference or a value.
- **Text under a DNP cross:** move the fields beyond the cross, which is wider than the symbol body.
- **Text on symbol outlines:** a field of one part over the body of another. Also pin names or numbers that cross
  their own symbol outline:
  - hide the pin names of op-amps (the triangle already shows + and −);
  - lengthen the pins whose numbers have three or more characters (e.g. `MP1`).
- **Sheet borders:** no text or wire may touch or cross the inner frame or sit in the border band with the zone
  letters and numbers. Keep a margin of at least one grid unit inside the frame.
- **Title block:** title and comment lines must fit inside the title block. Shorten them; put detail in a note on
  the sheet.

## 2. Wire passives to the parts they serve

- Resistors, capacitors, inductors, diodes and test pads that belong to an IC are **wired** to its pins.
  Examples: decoupling, feedback dividers, pull-ups, filters, gate and base resistors.
  **Never** connect them with two labels of the same name.
- Labels are used only for nets that leave the sheet, or for long buses where a wire would cross the drawing.
- Place each passive next to the pin it serves:
  - decoupling at its power pin;
  - feedback divider at the FB pin;
  - pull-up or pull-down on the line it holds.
- Two-pin parts sit in line with the wire (`span`), or hang from a wire node (`drop`).
- A wire must never run through the body of a part. That shorts the part: check it in the generator.
- A pin or label anchor must never land in the middle of a wire unless it is an intentional T-junction.
  Add a junction dot wherever three or more connections meet.

## 3. Text placement conventions

- **Two-pin parts:**
  - vertical body: reference and value beside the body;
  - horizontal body: reference above, value below;
  - never on the body and never on a neighbouring wire;
  - switch sides (left/right) when the default side collides.
- **Transistors:** text on the side opposite the gate or base.
- **ICs:** library field positions, unless they collide. Then move the fields above or below the body.
- **Test pads:** reference only; hide the "TP" value.
- **Power symbols:**
  - a ground that must sit above a part is drawn inverted, not with a wire looping around;
  - a rail symbol next to a test pad goes beside the pad, so that its name clears the pad.
- **Long values** (e.g. `10uF 50V`) need room. Stagger neighbouring capacitors or spread them, so that no value
  crosses the next part's wire.

## 3a. Mandatory sweep of every resistor and capacitor (reference and value vs. symbols)

Resistors and capacitors are the parts whose texts collide most often: they are many, small, packed in rows
(pull-down columns, decoupling banks) and their values grow (`4.7uF 50V`, `6.8nF C0G`). A visual review misses
the cases where the last letter of a value just touches the next part, and the PDF check (section 9) ignores
boxes that only touch. So, on **every sheet and every R\* / C\* part**, without exception:

1. Run `python3 scripts/fix_passive_text.py hardware/ --check --verbose`. For each visible Reference and Value it
   computes the text box with the full character advance (0.96 × font size per character, measured on KiCad
   plots) and reports every hit against: its own body (DNP cross included), another part's body or pins, a wire,
   a label, a junction, a no-connect, a note, another text, the frame or the title block.
2. Fix every hit:
   - **hand-drawn schematic:** `--apply` moves the texts to the first clear conventional position (vertical
     body: beside it, right then left, reference above value, then shifted by half-grid steps; horizontal body:
     reference above / value below, then both above, then both below, then shifted). Texts are written centred
     and horizontal, so rotation and mirroring cannot flip them. Only the `(at …)` of the moved fields, their
     `justify` and the symbol's `fields_autoplaced` flag change;
   - **generated schematic:** never `--apply`; fix the placement rule in the generator (text side, gap, the
     pitch of a column of parts) and regenerate.
3. **Unresolved** parts have no clear conventional position: the parts are too close (e.g. a pull-down column
   pitched below the width of `100K` plus the next body). Give them room — wider pitch, stagger, move the
   neighbour — never a smaller font and never a hidden field.
4. Re-run until the sweep reports 0 to move and 0 unresolved; then run the drawing check (section 9), ERC and
   the connectivity check (section 0). Add other prefixes (`--prefixes R,C,L,D,F`) when those parts are dense.

## 4. Labels sit on a wire

- Every net label sits on a wire that runs **under its whole text**. The wire starts at the connection point and
  ends at the far end of the label text; the label is anchored at that far end.
- Do not leave bare label text floating beyond the end of a short stub.
- Apply this everywhere: sheet labels, the labels in the ports block and the labels at the root-sheet pins.
- **Size the wire from the real text width.** KiCad's stroke font is proportional ("I" 0.61 mm, "M" 1.45 mm at
  1.27 mm): use `scripts/kicad_text.py` (`text_width`, `aligned_label_anchor`), never characters × a constant —
  that leaves the inner end of long names off by more than 1 mm and lets the text run past the wire.

## 4a. Align the labels of a column

A column of nets leaving a part (one label per pin or lane) must read as a column: **the texts start on one
vertical line next to the pins** (for a column on the left of a part, the texts end on one line next to the pins).
The reader scans the names from the part outward; ragged starts look misaligned even when the far ends line up.

How to do it with the label still anchored at the far end of a wire that runs under its whole text (section 4):

1. **Start every label wire at the same coordinate** — the column. Lanes that leave from a different point (after
   a pull-down node, after a series part) are extended to the column before their label. Exception: a part
   inline in the lane whose texts need the room of the neighbouring lanes (e.g. a series resistor between lanes
   at 2.54 mm pitch) keeps its label after the part; say so in a note.
2. **Compute the far end from the real text width:** `end = start + gap + text_width(net) + 0.23 mm` (KiCad draws
   the label text 0.23 mm inside its anchor). `scripts/kicad_text.py` → `aligned_label_anchor()`. The font is
   proportional: characters × a constant misplaces the start by more than 1 mm.
3. **Snap the far end to a 25 mil connection grid** and set the project's *Schematic Setup → Connection grid* to
   **25 mil**: it is KiCad's minimum — a smaller value (e.g. 10 mil) is silently raised to 25 mil, and ERC then
   reports `endpoint_off_grid` for every label wire it merges with a pin stub. Everything else stays on the 50 mil
   (1.27 mm) grid; only label-wire far ends use the 25 mil steps. Result: text starts within ±0.32 mm. Record the
   change of the reference project's connection grid as a deviation.

What does **not** work:
- **Aligning the far ends** (all wires of the column end on one line): exact on the grid, but the texts then
  start at different places and, where a lane continues past its label (to a pull-down column), the text floats
  mid-wire — reviewers read it as misaligned.
- **Anchoring the label at the column start** with the text running outward over a longer wire: every label
  wire gets a free end beyond the text → `unconnected_wire_endpoint` errors (115 on a real project).
- **Letting the text run past a short stub** with no wire under it: aligned, but breaks section 4.

Safety: lengthening a wire for cosmetic reasons (here: extending a lane to the column) must never let it reach
another wire, pin or label anchor — a label wire extended into the vertical wire of a neighbouring pull-down
column silently shorted CHRG, STDBY and CTP_RST on a real project. Run the connectivity check (section 0) after
every alignment change, then look at the rendered columns. In a generator, emit labels at the end of each sheet
so the alignment sees the whole drawing.

## 5. Sheet interfaces: a ports block on every sheet

- Every sheet carries a **SHEET PORTS** block: a dashed text box titled "SHEET PORTS", placed in a free corner.
  Inside it, one row per inter-sheet net, sorted by name and pitched at one grid step. Each row is:
  - a hierarchical label (with its direction shape: input / output / bidirectional);
  - wired to a local label of the same name.
- **The ports block holds real objects, never a written list.** A text box that only *names* the ports
  ("DUT_PWR_ON in, DUT_PWR_FLT out, +24V_IN (power symbols)") is not a port: KiCad cannot connect it, ERC cannot
  check it and the root shows nothing. Each signal row is a hierarchical label; each rail row is the rail's power
  symbol with a short "in" / "out" text beside it.
- **Every sheet declares its inputs and its outputs**, a power sheet included. A sheet symbol on the root with no
  pins (a power-input or power-rails sheet whose nets are all power symbols) hides the interface: give that sheet
  symbol a text below it, inside its own outline, listing the rails it takes in and gives out
  (`in: +24V_JIG, GND - out: +5V, +3V3, +/-15V`), and keep the rails' power symbols in the sheet's ports block.
- The keep-out of the block includes the hierarchical-label text, which extends beyond the anchor.
- Inside the sheet, inter-sheet nets use **local labels**. No global labels for signals.
- Power nets stay power symbols.
- On the root sheet, every sheet symbol shows its sheet pins. Each pin is wired to a local label of the same name,
  so that every inter-sheet connection is visible on the root.
- **The root reads as a block diagram.** Sheet symbols stand in signal-flow order, left to right (input -> rails
  -> processing -> outputs), sized to their pins, and where two sheets meet the pins are joined by a **wire** (a
  label pair only where a wire would cross the drawing). The reader must see which sheet feeds which.
- **A sheet pin never ends in a no-connect flag.** If the destination sheet is not drawn yet, create it now as an
  empty sheet with its ports and a "TBD - phase N" note, and wire the pins to it. A no-connect says "this signal is
  not used", which is the opposite of the truth.
- Net names of root-level nets gain a leading "/" in the netlist. Normalise them in the netlist rules instead of
  renaming.
- When two sheets are merged, nets that no longer leave the page lose their sheet port (they become
  `/<Sheet>/<NET>` in the netlist); update the PCB net names and footprint sheet paths in the same change so the
  board keeps schematic parity.

## 6. Sheet structure

- **Every sheet is drawn on A3, landscape** - the root sheet, every sub-sheet and every page of the PDF. A3 gives
  the blocks the room of section 8a.5 without shrinking text, and one paper size keeps the printed set uniform. A
  larger size (A2) only where a single block cannot fit on A3, recorded as a deviation; never A4. Set it in the
  generator or in *Page Settings* before placing anything: changing the paper later moves the title block and the
  frame over the drawing.
- **Identical blocks** (input channels, output channels) go **on one sheet**, one band per instance, references
  offset per instance (e.g. channel 2 = channel 1 + 100). Do not spread them across sheets with half-empty pages.
- **Small, related sheets are merged.** Each merged block keeps its own heading on the page (A3; see above).
  Examples:
  - battery + regulators;
  - display header + connectors;
  - MCU + its I/O expander; DAC + the output stages it drives.
- **A part with many outputs gets its own sheet** when it crowds another drawing, e.g. an I/O expander.
- **Connectors live on the connectors sheet** (signal connector, USB receptacle with its ESD and CC parts). Parts
  that must sit at the MCU pins (series resistors, DNP caps) stay on the MCU sheet; a pair of ports joins them.
- Remove the files of merged or renamed sheets. Update every note, block diagram and cross-reference that names
  them.
- Measure the blocks against the A3 usable width: if the widths of the blocks that must sit side by side exceed
  it, propose an arrangement (stack two blocks in one column, or split the sheet) instead of squeezing pitches.

## 7. Many-pin parts (expanders, connectors, MCU)

- Use a **5.08 mm pin pitch** symbol variant when passives must be wired to adjacent pins. Pins, names and
  footprint stay identical to the datasheet symbol; re-run the pin check.
- **Outputs with pull-downs or pull-ups:**
  - each output lane runs to its own column, the top lane outermost (staircase), so that no lane crosses another;
  - each column ends in a **vertical** resistor to the rail;
  - the column pitch must leave room for the value text beside each resistor (≥ body width + gap + value width;
    for a 0603-style resistor symbol and `100K` at 1.27 mm that is 1.14 + 0.6 + 4.9 + 1.14 ≈ 7.8 mm, so 4 grid
    units (10.16 mm) on a 2.54 mm grid; 3 units (7.62 mm) is too tight) — section 3a catches a pitch that is
    too tight;
  - the label sits on the lane near the pin.
- Inputs with no local parts get only a short wire and a label.
- Unused pins get no-connect flags.

## 8. Orientation and flow

- Signal and power flow left to right: inputs on the left, outputs on the right.
- Rotate modules and ICs so that their pins face where their nets go. For example, an isolated DC/DC module
  horizontal, with input pins on the left and output pins on the right.
- Supplies go up, grounds go down, except for the inverted-ground case above.
- Keep each block compact. Related parts sit together; free space goes to notes and the ports block.
- A block drawn from a datasheet's application circuit keeps the figure's arrangement through this pass: tidy its text and labels, but leave each part on the pin and in the order the figure shows (`reports/app_circuits.json`).

## 8a. Rules from the review of a generated schematic (VibraOne test jig, 2026-11-11)

A schematic generated with this skill (two boards, seven sheets) was reviewed sheet by sheet. Every item below was
seen on it; each one is a rule from now on. Sections 1-8 already cover some of them; these say how far to go.

1. **Ports on every sheet, with directions** - section 5: real hierarchical labels and sheet pins, rails declared
   on power sheets; no text-only "SHEET PORTS" list.
2. **Root as a block diagram** - section 5: flow order, wires between sheet pins, no pin ending in a no-connect.
3. **No text over anything** - section 1, applied to every field (reference, value, label, power-symbol name, pin
   name and number, note): a rotated value over its own symbol, a value across a connector box, a TVS value over a
   ground name, a part value over an IC's ground pins and their ground symbols, a stack of rotated references in a
   series-resistor bank - all seen, all failures.
4. **Short value, part number in a field.** The visible Value says what the part is (`10k`, `100nF 50V`,
   `PTC 1A 2512`, `Terminal 5P 5.08`, `eFuse`); the manufacturer part number lives in the `MPN` / `LCSC` fields,
   hidden. A part number as the value (`DB128V-5.08-5P-GN-S`, `TPS7A3001DGNR`, `ADP7142AUJZ-R7`) is too long for
   the space beside a symbol and crosses its pins. ICs may show their part name when it fits inside the body width.
5. **Room before tidying.** Spacing is set so the texts fit, not the other way round: 7.62 mm minimum between
   parallel two-pin parts in a bank (10.16 mm when the values are two words, e.g. `22uF 25V`), 10.16 mm between
   neighbouring ICs, and a series-resistor bank between labels and a connector at the connector's pin pitch only
   when the resistor texts sit outside the bank (otherwise spread the bank, or move the resistors to the far end of
   the lanes).
6. **Power-symbol orientation.** GND and negative rails point down, positive rails point up, everywhere, connector
   pins and module outputs included. A ground symbol rotated sideways or upward to save a wire is a failure; run a
   wire to a ground that points down.
7. **PWR_FLAG once per rail**, at the rail's source (connector or regulator output), in free space beside it. Not
   one on every branch, not in the middle of a drawing.
8. **A label or power symbol never sits on a pin.** It starts at the end of a wire stub of at least one grid unit,
   so the pin number stays readable (section 4).
9. **DNP parts** keep their fields readable: mark them with the DNP attribute (the value may read `DNP`); never let
   the DNP cross cover a neighbour's label or wire. Give a DNP part the same clearance as a fitted one.
10. **Identical blocks are drawn identically** (section 6): the same relative placement in every instance, and a
    reference offset per instance. Two copies that differ in layout make the reader look for an electrical
    difference that is not there.
11. **References follow the drawing.** Number left to right, top to bottom, within each block, and channels in
    channel order (R1 for channel 1 ...). A block numbered R4, R3, R2, R1 from left to right reads as wrong.
    Annotate by position after placement (KiCad: *Annotate, sort by X position*), or emit the references in order
    in the generator.
12. **Short notes.** A note on the sheet states what the reader needs at that spot: the formula behind a value, a
    pin that must stay open, a placement rule. Calculations, decision histories and file paths go to the report or
    the `calc/` script; at most one short source line on the sheet (`calc: ctrl_power_values.py`). Internal
    decision numbers (`D-xx`) and phase names are allowed only in the source line. Place the note next to the block
    it explains, never as a half-page box.
13. **Title block for the reader.** Title, sheet description in one line, revision and date (principle 8), owner
    and author. Process remarks ("generated by ...; do not edit by hand") go to the README, not the title block.
14. **Sheet pins whose destination is not drawn yet** - item 2: empty destination sheet with its ports, never a
    no-connect.
15. **Use the page.** No crowded cluster next to an empty half page: spread the blocks over the sheet, or split
    the sheet (for example controller and operator interface on two sheets) when a cluster cannot get its room.
    Signal-name suffixes that encode a convention (e.g. `_M` for the module side of a series resistor) are
    explained in a one-line note on the sheet.
16. **A3 landscape on every sheet** (section 6). The reviewed schematic was drawn on A4: the crowded clusters and
    the stacked texts of items 3 and 5 came from fitting the blocks into the smaller page.

## 9. Automatic drawing check (on the plotted PDF)

`python3 scripts/check_drawing.py hardware/<project>.kicad_sch [--allow docs/drawing_allow.csv]` exports the
schematic to PDF with `kicad-cli` and checks every page:

1. **Text/text:**
   - intersecting text boxes, slightly shrunk to ignore touching boxes;
   - ignore duplicate plotted text (KiCad plots each text twice).
2. **Text/wire:** a wire segment (wire colour) intersecting a text box.
3. **Text/DNP:** a DNP cross segment intersecting a text box.
4. **Text/body:**
   - a symbol-outline segment crossing a text box (stroke width included);
   - keep a short, documented allow-list (CSV: sheet, text, reason) for text that a library symbol draws inside
     its own artwork.
5. **Text/NC:** field or label text under a no-connect cross (pin numbers exempt).
6. **Frame:**
   - text or wire outside or crossing the inner frame, title-block text included;
   - zone markers in the border band are the only exception.

The check reports each hit with the sheet name and the position in mm, and fails on any hit. Because touching
boxes are ignored, it does not replace the R/C sweep of section 3a.

Use a free-area finder on a draft plot to place each ports block: a draft plot with the ports moved off-page, then
a search for an empty rectangle. Store the positions in the generator, so that they stay reviewable.

## 9a. Automatic structure check (on the .kicad_sch files)

`python3 scripts/check_structure.py hardware/<project>.kicad_sch [--max-value 12] [--json OUT]` reads the root and
every sub-sheet (no PDF, no KiCad needed) and checks the rules of sections 5 and 8a that a plot cannot show:

| Check | Level | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `sheet-no-pins` | FAIL | a sheet symbol with no pins and no `in: ... / out: ...` rails text inside its outline |
| `ports-text-only` | FAIL | a SHEET PORTS box that lists ports in text, or has no hierarchical label / power symbol under it |
| `pin-no-connect` | FAIL | a sheet pin on a no-connect flag |
| `label-on-pin` | FAIL | a label anchored on a pin whose number is shown (ICs, connectors) |
| `power-on-pin` | FAIL | a power symbol directly on a pin whose number is shown |
| `power-sideways` | FAIL | a power symbol pointing left or right |
| `power-inverted` | WARN | a ground / negative rail pointing up, or a rail pointing down (allowed: inverted ground above a part) |
| `pwr-flag-count` | FAIL | more than one PWR_FLAG on one net |
| `long-value` | WARN | a visible value longer than `--max-value`, or equal to the MPN (ICs whose value fits the body exempt) |
| `ref-order` | WARN | references of a prefix running right to left / bottom to top in a row or column |
| `long-note` | WARN | a note over 6 lines or 400 characters (ports block exempt) |
| `title-process` | WARN | a title-block comment with a process remark |
| `paper-size` | FAIL | a sheet not on A3 landscape (A2 / A1 / A0 reported as WARN: allowed as a recorded deviation) |

Pins with hidden numbers (resistors, capacitors) may take a label or a ground directly: the rule protects pin
numbers, and theirs are not shown. The check fails on any FAIL; every WARN is fixed or explained in the drawing
report. Self-test: `python tests/run_tests_structure.py`.

## 10. Verify and deliver

After each step, and before every commit:
- ERC: 0 errors, 0 warnings;
- annotation check: unique references;
- netlist rules;
- datasheet check;
- R/C text sweep (`fix_passive_text.py --check`): 0 to move, 0 unresolved;
- label columns aligned on their text starts (section 4a), checked on the rendered pages;
- drawing check (`check_drawing.py`): 0 hits;
- structure check (`check_structure.py`): 0 FAIL, every WARN fixed or explained;
- connectivity equivalence (`check_connectivity.py`): identical to the reference netlist.

Then:
- render the changed pages and look at them; the automatic checks do not see everything;
- commit with a message that lists the drawing decisions;
- record them in the decisions log;
- send the updated PDF.

<constraints>
- NEVER change connectivity, values, references, footprints or LCSC codes in a drawing pass. If a drawing change
  seems to need an electrical change, stop and ask.
- NEVER hide an overlap by shrinking fonts or hiding fields that carry information (reference, value). The only
  fields that may be hidden are the test-pad "TP" value and op-amp pin names.
- NEVER add an allow-list entry to the drawing check for an overlap you caused; fix the placement.
- NEVER edit generated schematic files by hand when a generator exists; NEVER run `fix_passive_text.py --apply`
  on them.
- NEVER leave an R/C sweep hit unresolved: make room.
- NEVER lengthen a wire (label alignment or any other cosmetic reason) without checking that it touches nothing
  else; re-run the connectivity check after every alignment.
</constraints>

<acceptance>
- Every sheet passes the drawing check with 0 hits and the R/C sweep with 0 hits, and a visual review finds no
  clipped, crossed or crowded text.
- Passives are wired to their parts; labels appear only on inter-sheet nets; every column of labels is aligned.
- Every sheet has a SHEET PORTS block made of real hierarchical labels and power symbols; every sheet symbol on the
  root shows its pins (or, for a power-only sheet, its rails in and out); the root reads as a block diagram in flow
  order, and no sheet pin ends in a no-connect.
- Values are short (part numbers in fields); grounds point down and rails up; one PWR_FLAG per rail; references run
  in drawing order; notes are short and sit next to their block (section 8a).
- Test pads show their reference only (the "TP" value hidden) on every sheet.
- Identical channels share a sheet; small related sheets are merged; connectors are on the connectors sheet.
- Connectivity is identical to the reference netlist.
</acceptance>
